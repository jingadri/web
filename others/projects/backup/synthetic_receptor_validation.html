Validate Before the Lab

How to raise the chance a synthetic NLR works, and cut wasted experiments, using published data. Companion to the workflow map (sections 11–14). Compiled 2 Oct 2026 from the literature; items marked unverified still need a check against the source.

How to read the evidence

Every statement on this page is only as strong as its support. Grades follow an audit of 79 statements against the source texts (before the fixes below: 25 read directly in a primary source, 21 from abstracts or secondary sources, 29 my inference, 4 unsupported or contradicted; the 4 are corrected).

A read in a primary source and matches   B abstract or secondary source   C my inference, plausible but untested   Unproven means not trusted: C items are hypotheses to test, not findings. Raw audit and claim tables are in the session scratchpad.

Is there a determinant of success?

Yes for autoactivity (the 67% failure); no for being functional (the ~4%).

Level of evidenceDeterminant
Backed by published studies

Established for “functional”None. The two functional binders look like the autoactive ones in sequence, and n is too small for statistics.

Contradiction: the FoSSP17 scoring used Pikp-2, the tolerant helper, yet 67–72% of binders were still autoactive, so the helper alone does not explain it for de novo binders. The paper gives no explanation beyond precedent.

Next step: get the full 46-binder table from the authors and test charge, polarity, epitope and termini geometry on all 46; the public Pik-1/Pik-2 allele scores (Xi 2026, Table S4) can already test the helper effect. Until then: test both helpers early, keep the HMA’s size and junction geometry, use low-dose expression, and do not rank by affinity.

Bottom line. In the closest precedent, de novo binders put into Pik-1 almost never worked: only 2 of 46 (FoSSP17) and 0 of 44 (AVR-PikF) were functional and about 67–72% were autoactive. Binding strength did not predict which ones worked. So the effort should go into filtering out autoactivity and choosing the helper and expression conditions, not into ranking by affinity. Computation can cut the wet-lab count; it cannot show signalling.

1The precedent: 46 de novo binders in Pik-1

Xi et al. 2026, bioRxiv 10.64898/2026.07.10.737686. Binders to FoSSP17, designed with RFdiffusion + ProteinMPNN (AF2 confidence metrics were used, but no thresholds or per-binder values are reported for FoSSP17; the 0.85/0.80/5 filter was stated for the AVR-Pii designs), replaced the Pikm-1 HMA domain (partial N- and C-terminal HMA sequence was kept in a new “LV2” Pikm-1 chassis, built to reduce autoactivity). The 46 came out of a large funnel: 19,200 backbones → about 192,000 sequences per target; top 5,000 by pAE went to partial diffusion (about 100,000 variants); about 10,000 to yeast display; then selection. Yeast display means the tested pool was already enriched for binding; the order of filters is not reported. The target was the AF2 monomer, but FoSSP17 is a homodimer.

Shared by all groups

Small helical bundles, nearly all acidic (net −1 to −10), 11 of 12 expressed in E. coli, all associate with the effector by co-IP. So binding, length and fold do not separate the outcomes.

Functional (2)

Moderate KD (1.3 and 13.9 µM), mid hydrophobicity. Fobinder1 contacts the effector’s α3/α5 helices, away from the dimer interface. Nothing else sets them apart from the autoactive binders in sequence terms.

Repressed (9; 4 named)

Three of the four named are near-neutral (net +1, 0, −1), unlike most others, and the group includes the tightest binder (0.48 µM). But Fobinder7 is −10, and Fobinder6’s hydrophobic % equals functional Fobinder1’s, so no clean pattern. Hypothesis only (n = 4).

No response (2)

Fobinder9 is the only strongly basic binder (pI 9.4, +5) and still binds with a KD of 13.5 µM, the same as functional Fobinder4. Binding without signalling is the “dead” case of workflow section 14.

Epitope face: a possible determinant, but not clean

AF2 models (Fig S13) put Fobinder1, 5, 6 and 9 on the effector’s free face, and Fobinder2, 3, 4, 7, 8, 10, 11 and 12 on the face occluded by the FoSSP17 dimer interface. The authors suggest this may account for why only the free-face binders (and F4, which is on the occluded face) behaved in SEC. Against signalling it does not separate the groups: functional F1 is free-face but functional F4 is occluded; repressed binders are on both faces; the no-response binder F9 is free-face. Fig S12 lists 20 Fobinder1 side chains in contact with FoSSP17; design vs crystal RMSD is 0.46 Å (F1) and 1.12 Å (F9). Lesson: design against the biological dimer, not the monomer.

What this tells us. At sequence level the groups overlap too much to build a filter. The missing information is structural: where the binder sits on the effector, how its termini sit in the fusion, and expression level. Those are what the extra data from the authors (section 6) would test.

BWhat a working receptor is supposed to be, and where de novo fusions fall short

Built from primary literature on well-studied receptors (ZAR1, Sr35, NRC2/NRC4, RGA5/RGA4, Pik-1/Pik-2). Grades as in the legend: A read in text, B abstract or review only, C my inference. Where nothing was found, it says so.

1. The receptor at rest

PropertyEvidenceGrade
Three-part core: the NB-ARC (NBD + HD1 + WHD) works as a nucleotide switch, ADP-bound = off, ATP-bound = on; the LRR helps autoinhibition; the CC domain signals.Review Plant J 2026 (10.1111/tpj.70702); Zdrzałek 2020: “C-terminal LRR domain has a role in auto-inhibition”B/A
Held inactive by contacts between its own domains: “In healthy plants, NLRs are bound to ADP and kept inactive through intradomain interactions.” In ZAR1 the LRR is positioned “to sequester ZAR1 in an inactive state”; in NRC2, dimers and oligomers stabilise the inactive state, and dimer-interface mutations enhance cell death.Ma 2024 Nature (10.1038/s41586-024-07668-7); Wang 2019 Science (10.1126/science.aav5868, abstract)A
Pik pair is built differently from RGA5/RGA4. In Pik, every domain of both Pik-1 and Pik-2 is needed, the two associate at rest, and the authors call it “receptor cooperation rather than negative regulation”. In RGA5/RGA4 the helper is autoactive and the sensor represses it.Zdrzałek 2020 (10.1371/journal.pone.0238616); Cesari 2014 (10.15252/embj.201487923, abstract)A
In Pik-1 the HMA sits between the CC and NB-ARC (residues 186–258 in Pikp). Natural integrated domains make up about 10% of NLR genes.Maqbool 2015; Förderer 2022 (10.1038/s41586-022-05231-w)A
Kept at low levels on purpose: loss of the HSP90–RAR1–SGT1 chaperone depletes NLR protein; ubiquitin ligases such as CPR1 degrade SNC1 and RPS2; “excessive NLR expression can lead to unintended autoimmunity”. An integrated domain can itself be a turnover handle (RRS1 WRKY ubiquitinated by RARE).Ma 2024; reviews PMC6032283, PMC11865428; Nat Commun 2025 (10.1038/s41467-025-57231-9). Pik-specific chaperone and turnover data: not foundA/B

2. Activation

StepEvidenceGrade
Recognition: direct (Sr35 binds AvrSr35 through its LRR), indirect (ZAR1 via RKS1/PBL2), or through an integrated decoy (Pik-1 HMA binds AVR-Pik).Förderer 2022; Wang 2019; Maqbool 2015A

4. Why natural integrated domains work

PropertyEvidenceGrade
They are baits: fused domains “overlap with previously identified pathogen targets”; integrated HMAs mimic host targets of effectors (OsHIPP19).Sarris 2016 (10.1186/s12915-016-0228-7, abstract); Bialas 2021A
Natural binding is tight: AVR-PikD binds Pikp-HMA at 31 ± 2 nM (SPR); weaker alleles gave apparent values of hundreds of nM and little or no response; AVR-PikC did not bind. All AVR-Pik alleles bind OsHIPP19-HMA at nanomolar affinity.Maqbool 2015; review PMC13331592 for OsHIPP19A/B
They are tuned to the rest of the receptor: the HMA “likely coevolved with other NLR domains to maintain immune homeostasis”. Several natural integrated domains have lost the ability to signal alone. Size and shape are critical: “It was important to make sure the size of the HMA … was identical … removal of a single residue (ΔG186) resulted in strong autoactivity”; a Pro252 change did the same. HMA β1, α2 and β4 residues affect helper compatibility, not only effector binding.Bentham 2023; Bialas 2021A
Where natural scaffolds were re-surfaced, effector recognition could be restored (e.g. Pik-1 HMA mimicking OsHIPP19 restores recognition of AVR-PikC/F). Per-construct success rates for such studies were not collected, so they cannot be compared quantitatively with 2 of 46.Maidment 2023; PNAS 2024 (10.1073/pnas.2402872121)B

Putting it together. A functional fusion has to pass several requirements at once: stay quiet alone (1), fit the slot (2) and the helper (3), bind (4), relay the change (5), at tolerable dose (6). Natural integrated domains pass them because they were selected over evolution. De novo binders were designed only for requirement 4, and the data show that is the one that mattered least. Illustration, not data: if four independent requirements were each met with probability p, the chance of all four would be p⁴, and 2 of 46 (about 4%) would correspond to p of about 0.45 each.
What this does not show. The requirements were not measured per binder, the requirements are not independent, and the weights are unknown. The strongest link is requirement 1, because it matches the data in two independent binder panels and in every other non-co-evolved domain studied. The rest are consistent with the data but untested. The practical lesson that follows, and is itself an inference: design for requirements 1–3 and 5, not only for binding (for example by keeping the natural HMA scaffold and re-surfacing it, matching size and termini, and testing helper alleles).

An unresolved tension. The FoSSP17 cell death was scored with Pikp-2, the tolerant helper, yet 67–72% of binders were still autoactive. The paper gives no reason beyond precedent and says affinity does not track phenotype. The cause is unmeasured here; candidates are in the mechanism section. So the helper cannot be the whole story for de novo binders. Binder-intrinsic properties or expression are the more likely cause, and neither is measured per binder in the paper.

4Rules to raise success and cut waste

  1. Plan for low single-digit success. 2 of 46 (FoSSP17) and 0 of 44 (AVR-PikF), about 2% pooled with a wide interval. Budget dozens of designs per target; at 2–4% that gives roughly even odds of one hit, not a guarantee (my arithmetic, not from a source).
  2. Do not assume binding metrics predict signalling. In the data available, binding did not predict outcome (Fobinder5, tightest, was repressed; Fobinder9 bound with no response). Per-binder ipTM/ipAE values are not published for FoSSP17, so their predictive value cannot be tested here. A KD of about 1–15 µM is a plausibility bound taken from the two functional binders (1.29 and 13.9 µM), not a validated gate.
  3. Screen the helper first. Test Pikm-2, Pikp-2 and at least one more Asp230 helper (Pikps-2 or Pike-2) with every fusion before spending effort on structures.
  4. Fusion geometry gate. Model the full chimera; require the binder termini to sit at roughly the HMA termini spacing, keep the HMA flanking residues, and reject clashes with CC/NB-ARC.
  5. Epitope gate. Design against the biological state of the target. FoSSP17 is a dimer, and 8 of the 12 characterised binders sit on the face its dimer interface occludes. Prefer free-face epitopes, but expect this to fix binding and SEC behaviour, not signalling.
  6. Low-dose, controlled assays. The precedent used OD600 0.5 for NLR and effector, 0.1 for p19, 4-week-old plants, 5 dpi, a 0–6 score, 3 biological replicates (Myc:FoSSP17 and AVR-PikD were lowered to 0.1 for westerns). Controls in the paper: empty vector, AVR-PikD, Pwl2. Suggested additions: wild-type Pikm-1 + AVR-PikD, a non-binding mutant, an empty-ID fusion, and a western for expression, so autoactivity is not read as success.
  7. Treat charge as a weak hypothesis. Track net charge and hydrophobicity of every tested binder so it can be tested on more than 12 (section 2).
  8. Log every outcome against every score (workflow section 12), so the filters improve with each round.

7What cannot be validated on a computer

  • Whether a given fusion is autoactive: no validated predictor exists. Public cell-death scores exist for Pik-1/Pik-2 allele pairs and HMA variants (Table S4 of Xi 2026, Bentham 2023), but none was found for de novo binder fusions beyond the 12 named FoSSP17 binders.
  • Effector-triggered signalling, Ca²⁺ flux and resistance in planta.
  • The effect of expression dose and the right helper for each fusion.

All 12 characterised Fobinders associated with FoSSP17 in co-IP (selected from 46), while only 2 of 46 signalled correctly. That suggests binding is not the limiting step here, but it is an observation on one target, not a general rule.

Computation raises the odds and shrinks the wet-lab count; tiers 6–8 stay. Caveat on sourcing: figures were taken from the preprint text and supplement and from other agents’ reads of papers; DOIs and PDB IDs were checked for resolution, but threshold values quoted from the literature are starting points, not guarantees.
